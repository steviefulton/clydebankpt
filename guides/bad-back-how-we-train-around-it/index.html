Bad back? How we train around it

How Sanctuary Fitness coaches people with a history of back pain: what we ask first, the movements that help, the ones we hold back, and when to see a physio.

By Stevie Fulton, head coach at Sanctuary Fitness, Clydebank

A bad back is the most common reason people give for not training, and one of the best reasons to start, done properly.

What we ask first

What happened, what makes it worse, what makes it better, and whether you have seen anyone about it. If there are red flags or it is acute, Stevie will point you to a physio or GP before you lift anything.

Movements that usually help

  • Hip hinges with a kettlebell, taught slowly, so the hips do the work and the back does not.
  • Carries: farmer carries and suitcase carries build a trunk that holds you up.
  • Rows and pulls for a stronger upper back.
  • Split squats and goblet squats for legs without spinal load.

What we hold back

Heavy back squats, conventional deadlifts from the floor, and anything ballistic until the basics are solid and pain-free. That can be weeks or months. Nobody is timed.

The honest bit

Most backs get better with graded, coached strength work and worse with rest and fear. But coaching is not physiotherapy. If you need a physio, you will be told.

Questions

Quick answers.

Can I do the 8-Week Package with a bad back?

Usually yes. Classes are scaled and Personal Training slots are used for the movements that need close coaching.

Should I see a physio first?

If the pain is new, severe, or comes with numbness or weakness, yes. Stevie will say so at the consult.

Will lifting make it worse?

Badly coached lifting can. Coached, progressed strength work is what most back-pain guidelines recommend.
