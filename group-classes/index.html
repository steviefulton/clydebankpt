The classes

What happens in each one.

Every class is 60 minutes, coached from start to finish, and scaled on the day. Beginners train alongside regulars at their own level.

Mon 5:40pm · Tue 6:00am

HIIT

Sixty minutes of high-intensity intervals. The fastest fat-loss session on the timetable, scaled to your level on the day.

Tue 5:40pm · Wed 6:00am

Barbell & Dumbbell

Coached strength work with barbells and dumbbells. Squat, press, pull and hinge, with your technique watched every session.

Wed 5:40pm · Fri 6:00am

Strength and conditioning for real life: carrying, lifting, moving well and building a body that holds up.

Sat 9:00am

Full Body Workout

The Saturday session. Everything in one hour, then the weekend is yours.

What happens in the hour

Minute by minute, roughly.

No two sessions are identical, but the shape is. Here is how the sixty minutes usually run, so there are no surprises on your first night.

HIIT

  1. 0 to 10Warm-up: joints, then a build-up round at half pace.
  2. 10 to 45Intervals: work and rest in rounds, every move with a low-impact option. You pick the version, I pick the clock.
  3. 45 to 55Finisher: one short, honest push. Then it is done.
  4. 55 to 60Walk it off, breathe, stretch what you used.

Barbell & Dumbbell

  1. 0 to 10Warm-up and empty-bar or light-dumbbell practice of tonight's lifts.
  2. 10 to 40Main lifts: squat, press, pull or hinge, in sets, weight added when the technique earns it.
  3. 40 to 55Accessory work: the smaller movements that make the big ones better.
  4. 55 to 60Cool-down and a note of what you lifted, so next week beats it.

Functional Fitness

  1. 0 to 10Warm-up: mobility and a light circuit to wake everything up.
  2. 10 to 45Stations: carries, kettlebells, TRX, sled-style pushes and pulls, rotating in small groups.
  3. 45 to 55Conditioning block, scaled to the person, not the clock.
  4. 55 to 60Stretch and out the door.

Full Body Workout (Saturday)

  1. 0 to 10Warm-up together. Saturday is the busiest room of the week.
  2. 10 to 50Everything: a strength piece, then a conditioning piece, upper and lower, in pairs or threes.
  3. 50 to 60Finisher, stretch, coffee if you are quick. Then the weekend is yours.

Coached, not just supervised

What makes these different from a leisure-centre class.

Your technique gets watched

The coach is not at the front doing the workout. He is walking the room correcting squats, presses and hinges before bad habits set in.

Same faces every week

The classes are small enough that people know each other. That is why people keep turning up at 6am.

Tied to a plan

Classes sit inside the 8-Week Package with Personal Training, a nutrition plan and measurements. They are not a random workout, they are part of a result.

Questions

About the classes.

How do I join the classes?

Classes are part of the 8-Week Package (£205), which gives you every class on the timetable plus small-group Personal Training sessions and a nutrition plan. Message Stevie to book a free consult and your first week.

How long is a class?

Sixty minutes, every class.

Which class should a beginner start with?

Any of them. Every class is coached and scaled on the day. If you are nervous, start with a Personal Training session mid-week, where the basics are taught in a smaller group, then join the classes.

Are there early morning classes in Clydebank?

Yes. 6am HIIT on Tuesday, 6am Barbell & Dumbbell on Wednesday, 6am Personal Training on Thursday, 6am Functional Fitness on Friday. Done and showered before most people are up.

What is the difference between a class and Personal Training?

Classes are the set 60-minute sessions on the timetable. Personal Training sessions are smaller coached sessions mid-week, mornings 6am to 10am and late afternoons 4pm to 7pm, booked around you, with more individual attention on technique and your own programme.
